What it does with "lovely jeans, worn a few times"

You type your rough notes for Vinted:

"levis 501s, 32 waist 30 leg, mid blue, worn a few times, bit of fraying on the left hem, £18 offers welcome"

↓ and gets back

The listing

Levi's 501 straight leg jeans mid blue W32 L30

Classic Levi's 501s in a mid blue wash, straight leg.

Condition: good. Light fraying on the left hem (photo 5). No other marks.

Size: W32 L30. Measurements (laid flat): waist [ ] cm, inside leg [ ] cm, rise [ ] cm.

Material: [from the label]. [Postage: when you post.]

For you

  1. Fill in brand, size, colour and condition fields: buyers filter on them.
  2. Photos: front, back, the red tab and waist label, the hem fraying close up with a coin for scale, the jeans laid flat with a tape measure.
  3. Measure before posting: the [ ] gaps are yours to fill. It won't guess.
  4. Offers: £18 with offers welcome. If someone offers £8, it'll draft a friendly counter at your floor price.

Everything comes from your notes. Gaps are marked, never made up.

The free skills

listing-writer

A title in the order buyers search (brand, item, style, colour, size), counted against eBay's 80 characters. Honest condition grade with each flaw's what, where and how big. Measurements, a phone-friendly description, Etsy's 13 tags or eBay item specifics, and a shot list for the photos.

buyer-messages

Lowball counters that don't insult, "still available?" replies that nudge a sale, polite no's to off-platform payment, late-parcel updates, and step-by-step help with "not as described" claims: compare with the listing, use the platform's claim flow, act before the deadline.

sales-records

Splits personal clear-out sales from trading using HMRC's own signs, totals sales, fees, postage and stock per platform for the tax year, compares the £1,000 trading allowance with your costs, explains the platform reports to HMRC, and lists the dates. Estimates, not tax advice.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. The three skills are free and open source (MIT). You need ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude; on a free plan, use the "Any chat app" route above.

Do I have to pay tax on what I sell on Vinted or eBay?

Not usually for your own unwanted things. HMRC's guidance says you're unlikely to pay tax when you sell personal items from your home, but if you buy or make things to sell at a profit you're likely to be trading. Trading income up to £1,000 a year is covered by the trading allowance; over that you register for Self Assessment. The sales-records skill sorts your sales, and sellertaxcalculator.co.uk gives a free estimate. This is education, not tax advice.

Vinted or eBay says it shared my details with HMRC. Do I owe tax?

Not automatically. Platforms report sellers who made 30 or more sales, or received about £1,700 (€2,000) or more, in a calendar year. HMRC says the report doesn't mean you owe tax: what matters is whether you were trading, and your profit for the tax year (6 April to 5 April). Source: gov.uk.

Do I have to accept returns as a private seller?

As a private seller in the UK you don't have to accept change-of-mind returns, but the item must match its description, and platform protections can still apply if it doesn't. Business sellers selling online to consumers have to offer statutory cancellation rights. Check the platform's help pages and Citizens Advice for your situation. The buyer-messages skill asks which you are before it drafts.

Will it write "Nike style" or "designer dupe" to get more views?

No. Using another brand's name to attract searches misleads buyers and gets listings removed. It writes real brand names only, and for designer items it suggests photographing labels and receipts instead of claiming authenticity.
