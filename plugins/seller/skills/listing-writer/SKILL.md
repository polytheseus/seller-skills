# Writing a listing that sells

Most listings that don't sell fail in the same few ways:

- **The title doesn't match what buyers type.** "Lovely jumper, worn twice"
  is invisible in search. "Uniqlo merino crew neck jumper navy M" is found.
- **The condition is vague or rosy.** Buyers who find an undisclosed flaw
  open a "not as described" claim, and the sale is lost anyway.
- **No measurements.** Sizes vary by brand and era. Measurements stop the
  "will this fit?" messages and the returns.
- **Photos that hide the item**: dark, cluttered, no close-up of the label
  or the flaw.

This skill writes the listing and tells the seller what to photograph. You
draft and the seller checks it against the item in their hand. Never invent
a brand, material, size, measurement or condition detail the seller didn't
give.

## Step 1: Get the facts

Ask only for what's missing. Sellers usually paste rough notes or describe
a photo; work from that.

1. **Platform(s)**: Vinted, eBay, Etsy, Depop, Facebook Marketplace or
   several. Each wants a slightly different shape (Step 5).
2. **What it is**: item type, brand or maker, model or style name, colour,
   size, material (from the label if possible), era for vintage.
3. **Condition**, honestly: new with tags, new without tags, very good,
   good, or "has flaws". List every mark, bobble, chip, scratch, missing
   part, smell, repair or fault, however small.
4. **Measurements** (clothes: pit to pit, length, waist, inside leg; items:
   height × width × depth, weight if postage depends on it).
5. **Why someone wants it**: what it's for, what it goes with, anything
   rare or sought-after about it. Original price only if the seller knows it.
6. **Price and postage**: their price, whether offers are welcome, and the
   parcel size. (Pricing help: point them to the fees maths in their
   platform's help pages. Don't make up market prices.)
7. **Seller type**: selling their own unwanted things, or running a small
   business (buying to resell, or making items to sell). This changes the
   returns wording (see the `buyer-messages` skill) and, for tax, see the
   `sales-records` skill.

If the seller says "just write it", draft with gaps marked like
`[size?]` or `[pit to pit?]` rather than guessing.

## Step 2: Build the title from search words

The title is mostly for search. Put the words a buyer would type first,
then the details that help them choose.

**Order (clothes):** Brand + item type + key style words + colour + size.
"Levi's 501 straight leg jeans mid blue W32 L30"

**Order (other items):** Brand/maker + model or product name + item type +
key spec + condition if it's a selling point.
"Le Creuset 24cm round casserole dish volcanic orange"

**Rules:**
- Use the words buyers use, not the seller's words: "puffer jacket" not
  "padded coat thing"; "Y2K" or "90s" only if it genuinely is.
- No filler: "lovely", "stunning", "L@@K", "must see", emoji, repeated words.
- Only real brand names. Never write "Nike style", "dupe", "inspired by" or
  a brand the item isn't: it misleads buyers and platforms remove listings
  that use another brand's name to attract searches.
- Respect the platform's title length (eBay allows 80 characters; Etsy
  allows more but shows only the start in search, so front-load the key
  words). Check current limits in the platform's help pages if unsure.
- Spell the size the way the platform's filters do (UK 10, M, W32 L30,
  EU 38).

## Step 3: Grade the condition honestly

Use the platform's own condition options where it has them. Then describe
the actual state in plain words, **flaws first**, so nobody can say they
weren't told:

| Grade | Use it when |
|---|---|
| New with tags | Never worn or used, original tags or packaging attached |
| New without tags | Never worn or used, tags removed |
| Very good | Used a little, no visible flaws |
| Good | Used, light signs of wear, described below |
| Satisfactory / has flaws | Visible flaws that are described and photographed |

**Flaw sentence pattern:** what + where + how big + photo reference.
"Small bobbling under the arms and a 1 cm pale mark on the left cuff
(photo 6). Otherwise very good."

Never write "perfect condition" unless it is. When unsure, grade one step
lower: buyers who are pleasantly surprised leave good reviews.

## Step 4: Write the description

Short, scannable, mobile-first. Most buyers read it on a phone.

```
[One line: what it is and why it's good]

Condition: [grade]. [Flaw sentence(s), or "No flaws I can find."]

Size: [label size]. Measurements (laid flat):
- Pit to pit: [ ] cm
- Length: [ ] cm
[fit note: "fits like a UK 10, slightly cropped"]

Material: [from the label]
[Brand/model details, era, what it goes with: 1–2 lines]

[Postage: when you post, how. Bundle discount if you offer one.]
[Smoke-free / pet-free home: only if true]
```

**Rules:**
- Facts the seller gave only. If they didn't give a material or
  measurement, leave the placeholder.
- One or two lines of "why you'd want it" is plenty.
- No pressure tactics ("selling fast", "last chance") unless true.
- No promises the seller can't keep (next-day dispatch, "authentic"
  without proof of purchase). For branded or designer items, suggest
  photographing labels, serial numbers and the receipt; don't claim
  authenticity yourself.
- Business sellers: don't write "no returns" for online sales to
  consumers in the UK; buyers have legal cancellation rights. Private
  sellers can say no returns, but the item must still be as described.

## Step 5: Fit it to the platform

- **Vinted:** title plus description; fill in brand, size, condition,
  colour and material fields fully because buyers filter on them. Mention
  bundles if the seller has bundle discounts on.
- **eBay:** fill in every relevant **item specific** (brand, size, colour,
  style, material, department): eBay search and filters lean on them. Pick
  the most specific category. Offer an item-specifics list alongside the
  title.
- **Etsy (handmade, vintage 20+ years old, craft supplies):** title,
  description, and **tags**: Etsy allows 13 tags of up to 20 characters
  each. Write 13 distinct multi-word phrases a buyer might search
  ("gift for gardener", "oak chopping board"), not single words repeated
  from the title. Fill attributes (colour, occasion, material).
- **Depop:** casual, short, style words buyers browse by ("y2k", "grunge",
  "cottagecore") only where they fit; a few relevant hashtags (Depop caps
  how many count, so check the current limit). Fill in brand, size and
  condition fields.
- **Facebook Marketplace:** shortest of all; lead with the item, price,
  collection area and condition. Add "collection only" or postage terms.

## Step 6: Photo checklist

Give the seller a numbered shot list for this item:

1. Whole item, front, on a plain background in daylight (main photo).
2. Back.
3. Label or maker's mark (brand, size, material).
4. Close-up of texture or detail.
5. Each flaw, close-up, with a finger or coin for scale.
6. Item in use or worn (if the seller is comfortable) or with a
   measuring tape laid across it.
7. For electronics: powered on, showing it works; model and serial label.

Tips: no flash; turn off filters; straighten and de-fluff clothes; clean
the item; show every flaw named in the description.

## Step 7: Output

Give, in this order:

1. **Title** (with character count for eBay or Etsy)
2. **Description**, ready to paste
3. **Platform fields**: item specifics (eBay), 13 tags (Etsy), hashtags
   (Depop), or the fields to fill (Vinted)
4. **Photo checklist** for this item
5. **Check before you post**: placeholders to fill, facts to confirm against
   the item, anything that might trip platform rules

If the seller gave several platforms, give one title and description per
platform.

## Quality checklist (run silently before answering)

- [ ] The title starts with the words a buyer would search, has no filler,
      and fits the platform's limit (count eBay titles: 80 characters max).
- [ ] Every flaw the seller mentioned is in the description, flaws first.
- [ ] No invented brand, size, material, measurement or claim of
      authenticity. Gaps are marked `[like this]`.
- [ ] No other brands' names used to attract searches ("style", "dupe").
- [ ] Measurements are in the format the seller gave (cm or inches), with
      how they were measured.
- [ ] Etsy: exactly 13 tags, each 20 characters or fewer, no duplicates.
- [ ] Returns wording fits the seller type (business sellers don't say "no
      returns" to UK consumers).
- [ ] Short enough to read on a phone.
